Your challenges

Does this sound like you?

Five situations I see in manufacturing, distribution and B2B services. Open the one that speaks to you.

“I know my revenue, not my margin per deal”

You don’t know which customers or which deals make you money. Margin shows up at the end of the quarter, too late to act.

See my answer

The data exists, spread across tools that don’t talk to each other. Nobody’s job is to connect them, so every meeting starts the same collection all over again.

A three-week diagnostic to find where the margin leaks, then a steering dashboard plugged into your current tools. The Monday review arrives ready, with the gaps and the decisions to take.

Within 90 days: your margin per customer and per deal visible every month, an alert as soon as a customer drops below your threshold, and a weekly review prepared in minutes.

I did this at a B2B SaaS company: a profitability and churn dashboard per customer, built in two weeks with AI on existing data, and reviewed by the executive committee.

“My teams are trying AI in every direction”

You hear about it every day. Everyone has their own assistant, a few trials impress in meetings, but nobody has time to move from idea to production. You don’t know what it costs, what it brings in, or where your data goes.

See my answer

The rules of the game are missing: no use case chosen for its value, no test before scaling, no one accountable.

I sort the ongoing trials, keep one or two cases worth money and put them into service in 6 to 8 weeks, checked on your real cases. Your data stays with you.

Within 90 days: one or two uses in service with their measured gain, the others stopped knowingly, and a simple rule for the next ones.

“My salespeople fill in the CRM instead of selling”

Prospecting rests on one or two people. Inbound requests wait, trade-show contacts sleep in a file, and the sales forecast doesn’t hold.

See my answer

A small team cannot handle content, campaigns, qualification and follow-ups by hand, however hard it works.

I structure sales (targeting, opportunity tracking, a weekly review) and set up AI-assisted prospecting: sequences, qualification, a CRM kept up to date without double entry. A person approves every message, and your teams keep selling.

Within 90 days: qualified requests passed on within 24 hours, a next step for every opportunity, and a forecast you can believe.

I did this at a B2B SaaS company: marketing taken over after a departure, a prospecting chain built with AI where a person approves every message. Regular inbound requests where there were none, and prospecting run by a single person.

“Our quotes take days”

Pricing rests on two or three people. Sales administration re-keys orders into the ERP, and after-sales searches for information in three tools.

See my answer

Your pricing know-how lives in the heads of a few experts and in old quotes. The ERP can record an order, not prepare an offer.

I don’t touch the ERP. I add a layer on top of what exists: a draft quote prepared from your history and your product sheets, the order picked up without re-keying, parts and after-sales found in one search. A person approves every message.

Within 90 days: a first flow in service, from request to quote, a response time measured before and after, and experts less tied up on simple cases.

I did this at Manitou Group, for 1,050 dealers and 500,000 part numbers: ordering portal, quote configurator and product catalogue connected to the ERP. Time to create a quote cut in half.

“Nobody dares touch our in-house app anymore”

A tool built in-house runs part of the business. It depends on one or two people, every change is scary, and nothing is documented.

See my answer

The risk isn’t the age of the tool. It’s that nobody can say precisely what it does anymore, so nobody can take it over.

I first check whether an off-the-shelf tool can replace it, or whether we can keep it and plug in on top. If it has to be rebuilt, I write with your teams the test cases that describe what it does, turn them into the specification, then the tool is rebuilt module by module. Old and new run side by side until every test passes.

Within 90 days: a tool described by its tests, a first module rebuilt and in service, and a dependency on a single person that starts to disappear.

I did this at a B2B SaaS company where I was Deputy CEO for five years: a product sold mostly as a service, industrialised into a full SaaS product, released to production every week, with AI built into the product.

“Everything still comes back to me”

Every decision waits for your green light. Your managers come to you with issues they could settle themselves, and strategy happens in the evening.

See my answer

Your managers are not the problem. Nobody has a written scope or a decision rule, so everything goes up to the only person with the full picture.

I take on your operations a few days a month. I write down who decides what with your managers, set up a short weekly meeting and run two projects from start to finish.

Within 90 days: fewer issues on your desk, a named owner for each project, and a monthly review where you decide on facts.

“My large customers get stuck on security and contracts”

The prospect is convinced, then their procurement, security and legal teams send questionnaires and clauses. The deal drags on for months, and nobody on your side has time to answer.

See my answer

Your contracts and your security were designed for small customers. A large account asks for written proof: security policy, service commitments, data processing agreement.

I prepare a reusable security file for questionnaires and bring your terms and conditions, SLA and GDPR agreement up to standard with your lawyer, who approves them. I did it for a SaaS company: security run to the ISO 27001 framework, with external audits, without certification, contracts fully reworked.

Within 90 days: answers ready for your prospects’ procurement, security and legal teams, and faster signatures.

“I want to open a country, but nobody has time to run it”

A promising trade show, a few prospects abroad, a partner offering to represent you. But the team is already full in France, and the project moves in fits and starts.

See my answer

Opening a country is a project like any other: it needs an owner, a budget, milestones and criteria to continue or stop. Without them, it costs time and brings nothing in.

I frame the opening with you (target market, adapted offer, contracts, partners and public support), then run the first months: trade shows, prospecting, partner follow-up. I spent 4 years in the United States, exhibited at CES and NRF, and worked with Business France on those shows as well as on prospecting in Spain.

Within 90 days: a costed opening plan, first qualified meetings on site, and a clear decision at the end of the quarter: speed up, adjust or stop.

Frequent questions

What I am often asked

Our ERP is old and our data is a mess. Is that a problem?

No. I don’t touch the ERP. I add light layers on top of what exists, using only the data needed for a specific case, for example your quote history. During the diagnostic, I check how to access it: API, database reads or exports.

We have an in-house app that is hard to maintain. Should it be rebuilt?

Not always. I first check whether an off-the-shelf tool can replace it, then whether we can keep it and plug in on top. If it has to be rebuilt, I pin down its behaviour with automated tests, and the new version is checked against those tests. I only rebuild what sets you apart.

My teams are already swamped. How much of their time will it take?

A few hours, to validate the ground reality. I carry the project. And I don’t add a tool for them to fill in: I take data entry off their plate.

Our IT provider or ERP vendor is going to add AI. Why you?

They deliver generic features, the same for all their customers. I start from your pricing and your margins, and I work with them so the tool serves your profitability.

Who does the work, and what happens if you are unavailable?

I lead each project until it goes live, with your teams and your current providers. To build, I rely on a partner team: you have one point of contact, me. Everything is installed on your side and documented. I then support it over time, and you remain free to hand it to someone else.

How many days a month?

A few days a month, set at the start, with a cap written into the contract. The level depends on what you hand over: steering (monthly committee, weekly follow-up, dashboard) or leadership, with projects run from start to finish.

How is this different from a consultant or an interim manager?

A consultant delivers recommendations. An interim manager replaces someone full-time for a few months. I lead your transformation on a fractional basis, with written goals each quarter, and I run the projects until they go live.

Why not hire a full-time executive?

Hiring takes several months, and a full-time executive costs over €150k a year with employer charges in France. I am operational from the first month, a few days a month, and you adjust or stop at the end of each quarter.

What if it doesn’t work?

Each quarter ends with a written review against the baseline figures. You can stop at that point. What has been put in place stays with you, documented.

Do you also work beyond AI?

Yes. Once trust is there, the engagement can extend to fractional general management: organisation and delegation, large-account contracts, opening a country. For companies under €10M, I also work as a fractional COO.

How much does it cost?

The diagnostic has a fixed price, the same for every company. Beyond that, the price depends on the scope you hand over. After a first 30-minute call, you receive a written proposal within 48 hours.

Do you work with our tools?

Yes. I start from your current tools and impose no software.

What happens to our data with AI?

It stays in your database, with your hosting provider. If an external AI model is used, you know beforehand, and its contract forbids reuse of your data. Every AI tool is tested on your real cases before it is opened to the team, and passes seven guardrails before go-live. For each tool, you know in writing where your data is stored and in which country the model runs. When your data requires it, we pick a model hosted in the European Union. See the details

Do you work remotely and internationally?

Yes. I am based in Nantes, France, and I work in France and internationally, in English and in French. I travel on site, to Paris, other regions or abroad, for the days set each month.
